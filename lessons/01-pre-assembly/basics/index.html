Lesson 1 · the basics

Pre-Assembly Read Profiling: the basics

The main ideas in a few short blocks, with things to try. The full lesson has the details, commands and sources.

1A read is cut into k-mers

A read is the sequencer's readout of one piece of DNA. A k-mer is any stretch of k bases inside a read. Slide a window of k bases along the read, one base at a time, and note what is inside each time.

Top line: the read. Each line below: one k-mer.

2The histogram peak shows the coverage

The k-mer histogram shows how many different k-mers were seen once, twice, three times and so on. Coverage is how many times an average base of the genome was read. Each k-mer of the genome turns up a little less often than that, because a k-mer must fit wholly inside a read, so the bars pile into a peak that moves right as coverage grows.

3Errors make a tall spike on the left

A sequencer sometimes reads a base wrongly. A k-mer with the wrong base exists nowhere in the genome, so it is seen only once or twice. These k-mers pile up at the far left in the error spike, which is so tall that the plots cut it off.

all k-mers, those with errors in the spike on the left

4Total k-mers divided by the peak: genome size

Each position of the genome starts one k-mer, seen about as often as the peak says. So the genome size is about the total of all sightings divided by the peak position. We leave out the error spike, left of the valley (the low point between spike and peak).

left of the valley: not countedvalley and right of it: counted

5Differing chromosome copies make a second peak at half coverage

A diploid animal has two copies of every chromosome (one from each parent), not identical. Where they differ, each copy has its own k-mer, seen only in reads from that copy: about half as often as a k-mer both copies share. The share of differing positions is the heterozygosity. The more differ, the bigger the second peak.

k-mers present on both copiesk-mers present on one copy only

6A repeat is seen once per copy

A repeat is a sequence that occurs in several places of the genome. Its k-mers are the same in every copy, so each collects the reads of all copies: two copies, twice as often; four copies, four times. Here one piece is present once, twice and four times.

k-mers of the rest of the genomek-mers of the piece that is copied
