Lesson 5 · the basics

Population History from Many Genomes: the basics

The main ideas in a few short blocks, with things to try. The full lesson has the details, commands and sources.

1More genomes make many more pairs to compare

An animal carries two copies of each chromosome, so one genome gives one pair of copies to compare. Many animals give many copies, and every copy can be compared with every other. Each dot is one copy and each line is one pair.

Pairs grow much faster than animals, so a sample can ask who is related to whom, and how recently.

2Neighbouring stretches of a chromosome have different trees

Trace the copies of one stretch of chromosome back in time and they join at common ancestors. The result is a tree, the family tree of that stretch. At a recombination, an offspring chromosome switches from one parental chromosome to the other. The ancestors differ on each side, so the tree differs.

3More recombination means more trees

Each stretch between two recombinations has its own tree. The Y chromosome, over most of its length, and the mitochondrial DNA do not recombine, so each has a single tree. Below, one stretch is drawn with 0, 4 and 16 recombinations. Colour changes mark a new tree.

A single tree is a single draw of history, however many bases it covers.

4A long shared segment means a recent common ancestor

Two animals can hold the same stretch because both inherited it, uncut, from one common ancestor. Such a stretch is an IBD segment (identical by descent). Each generation back gives recombination another chance to cut it. Below, a common ancestor 3, 10, 30 or 100 generations back; every block is a piece never cut.

Length is in cM. One draw varies a lot, so a single segment cannot give the age; a whole set can.

5A small population shares many more long segments

In a small population two copies are more likely to share a recent ancestor, so pairs share more long segments. Ne, the effective population size, is the size of an ideal population that loses diversity at the same rate, and it is usually well below the number of animals. Each panel shows the expected segments of each length shared by one pair.

Counting segments of each length across a sample reads recent population size. Toy numbers, log scale.

6A SNP array sees only part of the variation

Many large samples were read on a SNP array, a chip that reads one fixed list of variable positions, usually the common ones. The minor allele frequency (MAF) of a site is the share of chromosomes that carry its rarer version.

Grey: all sites, as sequencing sees them. Coloured: sites an array keeps at a minimum MAF. Rare variants are mostly missed.
