08A complete SINE study, step by step

The earlier sections each took one piece: what a SINE is, how to find its copies, how to discover new ones, how to split them into subfamilies, how to compare two species, how to keep satellites apart. This section puts the pieces in the order our lab uses them, says which tool does each step, what goes in and what comes out, and what a finished study has to hand over.

What a finished study delivers

Our pipeline SINEderella states its aim in its own documentation (docs/AIM.md). We study interspersed repeats: elements whose copies sit in unique flanks. Copies that were duplicated together with their neighbourhood (a segmental duplication, a unit of a tandem array, an insertion hot spot) are not the subject: they are counted at most, never shown. The finished result has two parts:

  1. The technical result: the list of SINE loci of the genome, with a verdict for every copy and its family and subfamily.
  2. A page people can read: an HTML report with links to the alignments, conclusions on parts of the alignments, copy numbers, leak and divergence from the consensus.

Both are made by the workflow itself, on every run, without manual checking. That is the reproducibility requirement: if you run it again on the same genome and the same consensus bank, you get the same list and the same page. A person still makes the decisions (is this a SINE, where does it end, is this a subfamily), but always by looking at alignments the workflow produced, never at a number alone.

The steps

  1. Collect the known SINEss01
    Take consensus sequences of SINEs already described for your group and its relatives from SINEBase (Vassetzky and Kramerov 2013, Nucleic Acids Research 41:D83; it gives formal definitions of a SINE and classifies families by their parts) and from Dfam, plus consensuses from papers. This is your bank.
    in: literature, databases · out: bank.fa
  2. Scan the genome for new SINEss03
    SINE-de-novo-genome-scan cuts a SINE database into short pieces and searches each piece against the genome with ssearch36; each hit is widened by 50 bp on both sides and overlapping hits are merged into candidate loci, so hits up to about 100 bp apart join (the README says 500 bp; the code uses the 50-bp padding, s03). The idea: most SINEs share at least a little sequence with some known SINE. To look past the families you already know, mask their copies first and scan what is left (the minus bank).
    in: genome, SINE database · out: merged_loci.fa (candidates)
  3. Compress the candidates and looks04
    SubFam orders thousands of copies so that similar ones sit together, cuts the order into chunks of 50 and writes one consensus per chunk. A sample of 30,000 copies becomes about 600 rows that a person can read in ViewAlign, the lab's alignment viewer. SubFam only orders and averages; deciding which rows form a family or subfamily is the person's call.
    in: candidates (sample) · out: chunk-consensus alignment
  4. Set the boundaries by unique flankss03s05
    A SINE ends where its copies stop agreeing with each other. Past that point, the sequence of independent insertions is flank, and flanks of different copies should be no more alike than two random pieces of the genome. A consensus is built from the copies between those ends (for example with SINE_consensus).
    in: copies with flanks · out: new consensus
  5. Check the heads01
    Is the 5′ part derived from a tRNA, a 5S rRNA or a 7SL RNA? SINEderella's tools/trna_heads.sh compares every consensus with a set of tRNA genes, and compares 20 shuffled versions of it the same way; a head counts only when it scores better than every shuffle. It also applies SINEBase's rule (at least 60 % identity over at least 60 nt), and can add Rfam and tRNAscan-SE. Degraded heads are often missed by the last two; "not detected" is not "not tRNA-derived".
    in: bank.fa, tRNA gene set · out: head origin per consensus
  6. Collect every copys02
    SINEderella step 1 runs sear with each consensus: the genome is cut into 2,500 bp windows (2,000 bp step), and a hit is kept when it covers at least 80 % of the consensus at at least 65 % identity (sear -k query genome 0.8 65 50). Hits of all consensuses are merged into loci. Satellite-like hits are screened out here (s06, s07).
    in: genome, bank.fa · out: merged loci (BED), copies (FASTA)
  7. Assign copies to subfamiliess04
    Step 2 (asSINEment): 10 rounds of ssearch36; a copy is assigned only when the same consensus wins all 10, and its bitscore reaches 0.45 × that subfamily's N-th best score. Step 3 adds per-copy similarity to the consensus, leak and conflict flags, and a soft assignment for copies that missed the strict rule (nothing is thrown away).
    in: copies, bank.fa · out: assignment table
  8. Give every copy a verdicts05
    Flank tests decide what counts as an interspersed copy. Per subfamily, step 7 widens the flank window in 50 bp steps (up to 1 kb) until copy pairs are no more alike there than random genome pairs. Per copy, a flank-twin check compares each copy's flanks with those of the other copies of its family. Result, in loci_verdict.tsv: SINE (assigned, flanks unique), grey (flanks could not be tested) or not_SINE.
    in: assignment table, genome · out: loci_verdict.tsv
  9. Make the report and decide on alignmentss04
    Step 4 draws divergence histograms against the consensus; step 8a builds, per subfamily, an alignment of the 100 best and 100 random SINE loci with 50 bp of left and 70 bp of right flank, consensus on the top row; step 6 writes report.html with copy numbers, leak, divergence, head origin and a ViewAlign link for every alignment. You open the alignments and judge: real subfamily or mixture, right ends or not.
    in: run folder · out: report.html, alignments
  10. Compare species: orthologous locis05
    With a second genome, SINE_orth_loc maps the flanks of each copy into the other genome and sorts the locus pairs into SINE, PM and MP. In its bank mode, the SINE loci come straight from SINEderella's verdict table (sinederella_bed.py); only copies with verdict SINE are used.
    in: two genomes, two SINEderella runs · out: orth_sp1-sp2.tsv, alignment bundles
  11. Loop back
    A new consensus from the minus-bank scan or from a mixed subfamily goes back into the bank: SINEderella --add new.fa searches only the new consensus and re-assigns the copies it touches.

Where do you start? A step tracker

Not every study starts at step 1. Pick what you have, and the tracker lists the tool chain in order, with the files each step needs and makes. Tick a step when it is done; the tracker shows what you hold and what comes next. "What if I skip it?" says what goes wrong without that step.

Simple explanation with examples: why this order?

Think of a museum collection of beetles. First you read the literature (step 1: known SINEs). Then you sort through unlabelled drawers for anything beetle-like (step 2: de novo scan). You lay specimens out in rows so similar ones are side by side (step 3: SubFam), and decide where one species ends and another begins (step 4: boundaries). Only then do you count every specimen of each species in all the drawers (steps 6-7), and you drop the ones that are just duplicate photographs of the same specimen (step 8: shared flanks = one insertion copied twice).

Doing it in another order costs you. If you count copies before the boundaries are right, the consensus is too long or too short and copies are missed or merged. If you skip the flank test, a segmental duplication carrying 20 copies of one SINE looks like 20 independent insertions and inflates the count of a "young" subfamily.

Example from the tracker: start with "Two genomes" and skip the verdict step. The orthology run then also gets copies whose flanks are shared with other copies; their flanks can map to several places in the other genome, and the presence/absence call is less reliable.

The commands, in order

Each tool's own README has the full options. File names below are placeholders; the sample of 30,000 copies with a fixed random seed is what our pages use.

# 2. de novo scan (SINE-de-novo-genome-scan): shred a SINE database, drop low-complexity pieces, scan
bash 01_sanitize_shred.sh SINEBase.nr95.fa SINEBase.nr95
bash 02_filter_lc_cluster_nr85.sh SINEBase.nr95.fragments.norm.fa 8
bash sine_scan.sh genome.fa SINEBase.nr95.fragments.nr85.fa      # writes sine_search_out/<db>/merged_loci.fa

# 3. a fixed-seed sample of 30,000 candidates, compressed by SubFam into ~600 chunk consensuses
seqkit shuffle -s 42 merged_loci.fa | seqkit head -n 30000 > sample30k.fa
./SubFam.sh -n 50 -t 16 -o subfam -x cand sample30k.fa          # open subfam/cand.aln.fasta in ViewAlign

# 6-9. the whole SINEderella run on the curated bank, with the report and alignments
export SPECIES_CODE=mysp                                         # prefix of the published files
# web address where the alignment files will be published; the ViewAlign links on the page point there
export RAW_ALN_BASE=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/you/repo/main/mysp/alignments/
export TRNA_REF=trna_genes.fa                                    # tRNA gene set for the head check; without it that check is skipped
SINEderella --publish genome.fa bank.fa                          # results/report.html

# 10. two species: SINE loci from each run, then orthologous loci (bank mode)
sinederella_bed.py RUN_A -o aaa-BANK.bed
sinederella_bed.py RUN_B -o bbb-BANK.bed
SINE_orth_loc.bash aaa.bnk bbb.bnk BANK.fa                       # genomes named aaa.bnk, bbb.bnk

# + (loop back) add a consensus found later, without repeating the search for the others
SINEderella --add new.fa --run run_20261031_120000
In our projects

Our work on species A, a rodent, and a close relative walks most of this chain for one tRNA-derived SINE family. Known SINEs: a first bank of the 58 SINEBase consensuses classed as mammalian, then a bank for the family found (its SINEBase consensus plus four subfamily consensuses from the literature). Collect: more than a million loci in each genome, about five in six of them firmly assigned, most to one subfamily. Minus bank: with those loci masked, the de novo scan of the whole remaining genome took about a tenth of the time projected for the full genome and gave tens of thousands of candidates; a 30,000-copy sample went through SubFam into 600 rows per species. That de novo part is marked in our log as preliminary and still waiting for manual review. Orthology: several hundred thousand locus pairs, most with the SINE in both species and the rest split about evenly between the two. Two honest limits: whether a one-species locus is a new insertion or a deletion is not settled (a third, more distant species is the intended outgroup), and this orthology run took all firmly assigned copies, because it was made before the per-copy flank verdicts that bank mode now uses.

Reproducible on purpose

  • The page is an output, not a write-up. report.html, the alignments and their ViewAlign links are written by the run itself. Nobody edits numbers into it by hand.
  • Random choices are seeded. Samples for SubFam use a fixed seed in our runs; the random genome windows that set the flank background in step 7 are drawn with a fixed seed in the code.
  • Alignments repeat exactly. MAFFT's iterative refinement gave a different alignment on every multi-threaded run; SINEderella now passes --threadit 0 to every such call, which gives the same alignment for any thread count (from its PUBLISH_WORKFLOW.md).
  • Scores that wobble are filtered, not trusted. ssearch36 shuffles sequences to estimate its statistics, so bitscores change a little between runs. The 10-out-of-10 vote keeps only copies whose subfamily does not depend on that wobble (s04).
  • Record what ran. SubFam writes its version and command line into its log; our project logs record the repository commit of each run. SINEderella's manual also describes exporting run results to the public SINEdb pages.
Scope of the examples

The numbers in this lesson come from a few SINE families in a few genomes: the tRNA-derived Tal SINEs of talpids (moles, desmans, shrew moles), one SINE family in two rodents (species A and a close relative), a few lab tests on other genomes that are marked as such where they appear, and the published examples cited in each section. Thresholds such as 65 % identity, 0.8 of the consensus length, chunks of 50 and the 0.45 score factor are the tools' defaults, and these are the families we have used them on. For other SINEs they are a starting point, not proven settings: look at the alignments of your own family before you trust any of them.
