1A new copy duplicates a few bases beside it

A SINE (short interspersed element) is a short piece of DNA that codes for no protein of its own. The cell copies it into RNA, and a LINE writes that RNA into the DNA at a new place. A copy has a head, a body and a tail. At insertion the DNA is cut at two places a few bases apart, and the stretch between the cuts ends up on both sides of the copy: a target site duplication.

2The copy repeats; its two sides occur once

Interspersed means the copies are scattered, each between two pieces of DNA that occur nowhere else: the flanks. The computer searches a toy genome with six copies for a 16-base word from the SINE and for the 16 bases on each side of every copy (3 differences allowed).

3Many copies together give back the source

Each copy is made from a source sequence and then changes on its own, in different places. In an alignment, copies lie one under another with equal positions lined up. Taking the base most copies have at each position gives the consensus. Here 31 toy copies come from a known source, each changed at about 10 to 25 % of its positions.

4Group copies by shared changes, not by their number

Some changes arose once, in a source copy, and every copy made from it carries them. Copies sharing such changes form a subfamily; the positions where it differs from the other group are its diagnostic positions. Other changes are private to one copy. A dot means the same base as the consensus of all 15 copies; a letter is a difference.

5The same place in two species means one ancestor

A copy inserted once stays at its place in every descendant, and the flanks (block 2) find the place. At one place a genome either has the copy (1) or has the two flanks joined (0). Here a toy tree of species A, B, C has an outgroup O, a species outside the group.

6In a satellite the copies sit side by side

A satellite is one short sequence, the monomer, repeated head to tail; a run of monomers is an array. A dot plot compares a sequence with itself: a dot at (x, y) means the 8 bases starting at x equal the 8 starting at y. Both toy sequences have a unit of 24 bases.
