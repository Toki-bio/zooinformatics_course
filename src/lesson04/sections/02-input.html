02Preparing the input: one diploid genome

PSMC does not read your reads, your VCF or your assembly. It reads one long string of letters, one letter per 100 bases, that says only "here the two chromosome copies differ", "here they do not", or "here we do not know". Everything you decide while making that string (which reads count, which bases you trust, which chromosomes you leave out) goes straight into the curve. This section builds the string step by step, with the exact commands and defaults of the tools, and shows with a toy genome how loose filters invent heterozygous sites and strict ones throw real ones away.

What the input is

Diploid consensus
One sequence per contig in which every base is written as what the animal carries on both copies. Where the two copies differ, the base is written as a two-base IUPAC code (an A/G heterozygous site becomes R). Bases you do not trust are written in lower case or as N.
Callable bases
Positions where the reads were good enough that a heterozygous site, if present, would have been seen (lesson 3, s09). PSMC treats every base outside them as missing, not as homozygous.
psmcfa
The PSMC input file, written by fq2psmcfa. It looks like FASTA, but each letter stands for a 100-bp bin: K = at least one heterozygous site in the bin, T = no heterozygous site, N = (almost) no callable bases. The K here has nothing to do with the IUPAC K (G or T); it is just the letter Heng Li chose.
diploid consensus ACGTTAGCRTTAGGCATCCAG... nnnnnnnnnnnnnnnn... GGCTTA... psmcfa (100-bp bins) K N T
Simple explanation with examples: why PSMC only needs "differ or not" per 100 bases

PSMC looks along the genome for how far apart the differences between your animal's two chromosome copies are. Where the two copies trace back to a common ancestor a long time ago, mutations have had time to pile up, and differences are close together. Where the common ancestor is recent, differences are rare, so long stretches have none. The pattern of "dense" and "sparse" stretches is what PSMC turns into a history (s03).

For that it does not need to know which bases differ, only roughly where. So the genome is cut into 100-base boxes and each box gets one of three marks. In a mammal with about 2 differences per 1,000 bases, most boxes are T (no difference) and about one in five to ten is K. A box you could not read gets N, and PSMC simply skips it.

The danger is a box with the wrong mark. A sequencing error or two merged repeat copies turn a T into a false K: the animal looks more diverse, and the population looks larger. A real difference that your filter removed turns a K into a false T: the population looks smaller. A missing box marked T instead of N has the same effect. Good input means: as few wrong marks as possible, and as many honest Ns as needed.

The workflow

  1. Map the reads
    Reads of the animal onto its own primary assembly (or a close reference).
    minimap2 · bwa-mem2 · samtools
  2. Mark duplicates
    For PCR-amplified short-read libraries: copies of one fragment must count once.
    samtools fixmate / markdup
  3. Call genotypes at every base
    Either the PSMC route (bcftools consensus caller, all sites) or a modern caller plus a separate mask.
    bcftools mpileup | call -c · DeepVariant · Clair3
  4. Decide which bases are callable
    Depth between about a third (or half) and twice the mean, mapping quality, repeats and low complexity, sex chromosomes and mitochondrion out.
    vcfutils.pl vcf2fq · mosdepth · bedtools
  5. Write the diploid consensus and the bins
    IUPAC codes at heterozygous sites, lower case outside the mask, then 100-bp bins.
    vcf2fq · vcf2snp.pl + seqtk · fq2psmcfa
  6. Check what is left
    Share of bins that are N, heterozygosity, the X control, a second filter set.
    grep / awk on the psmcfa

1. Mapping and duplicates

Mapping and variant calling for one genome were covered in lesson 3, s09; use the same BAM. Two points matter here. First, map to a purged primary assembly (lesson 2, s06): if both haplotypes of a region are still in the assembly, each copy collects the reads of one haplotype and looks homozygous, which PSMC reads as a recent common ancestor. Second, in short-read libraries made with PCR, several reads can be copies of one DNA fragment. Mark them with samtools fixmate -m and samtools markdup (command in lesson 3). Both later tools skip reads flagged as duplicates by default: bcftools mpileup skips UNMAP,SECONDARY,QCFAIL,DUP, and mosdepth excludes flag 1796 (the same four).

2. Route A: the PSMC README route (bcftools + vcf2fq)

The PSMC README makes the diploid consensus in one pipe. Its example uses the 2011-era syntax (samtools mpileup … | bcftools view -c); with current bcftools the same thing is bcftools mpileup | bcftools call -c.

# bcftools 1.25, PSMC (lh3/psmc, 2025). Example for a mean depth of 30x: -d 10 (a third), -D 60 (twice)
# autosomes.bed = the autosomal contigs only (no X/Z, Y/W, mitochondrion); a .bed file is read as 0-based
bcftools mpileup -R autosomes.bed -f asm.fa -Ou aln.bam \
  | bcftools call -c -Ov \
  | vcfutils.pl vcf2fq -d 10 -D 60 | gzip > diploid.fq.gz

fq2psmcfa -q20 diploid.fq.gz > diploid.psmcfa
  • Why call -c and not call -m. vcf2fq reads three fields of the INFO column (where the caller writes extra values for each position) of every line: FQ (is the site heterozygous, and how sure), MQ (the RMS mapping quality) and DP (depth). In the bcftools source, FQ is written only by the original consensus caller (-c); the newer multiallelic caller (-m) writes MQ but no FQ, so its output does not fit vcf2fq (our reading of both sources; we did not run this combination).
  • All sites, not just variants. Do not add -v to call. vcf2fq needs a line for every position; positions without a line (no reads) become n.
  • What vcf2fq does (defaults -d 3 -D 100000 -Q 10 -l 5): a base is written in upper case only if its depth is between -d and -D and its RMS mapping quality is at least -Q; otherwise it is written in lower case, which fq2psmcfa treats as missing. Five bases (-l) on each side of a called indel are also put in lower case. A heterozygous SNV is written as its IUPAC code. The quality line of the FASTQ is the consensus quality.
  • Depth rule of thumb (README): "set -d to a third of the average depth and -D to twice". Measure the mean on the autosomes only.
  • Two traps. bcftools mpileup keeps at most 250 reads per position (-d 250), so for very deep data raise it above twice the mean, or the upper depth bound never acts. The README example also uses -C50; the current bcftools manual says it does "not recommend use of this parameter unless you fully understand the impact of it", so we left it out.

3. Route B: a modern caller plus a callable mask

DeepVariant (Illumina, HiFi, ONT) and Clair3 (ONT, HiFi) call long reads better than bcftools (lesson 3, s09 has their commands; for any non-human genome Clair3 needs --include_all_ctgs). Their VCFs list variants, not every base, so the mask has to come from elsewhere: a depth/mapping-quality BED from mosdepth, minus whatever else you exclude. The PSMC README shows the way for VCF + BED input (written for dipcall, a tool that calls variants by comparing two assembled haplotypes with a reference, but it works for any one-sample VCF): put the heterozygous sites into the assembly as IUPAC codes, put everything outside the mask in lower case, then bin.

# 1. callable mask: mosdepth 0.3.15, MAPQ >= 20, depth 10-60x for a 30x mean (bins are half-open: 10:61 = 10..60)
export MOSDEPTH_Q0=LOW MOSDEPTH_Q1=CALLABLE MOSDEPTH_Q2=HIGH
mosdepth -n -Q 20 --quantize 0:10:61: -t 4 cov aln.bam
zcat cov.quantized.bed.gz | awk '$4=="CALLABLE"' | grep -v -w -f sex_and_mt_contigs.txt > callable.bed
bedtools subtract -a callable.bed -b simple_repeats.bed > mask.bed      # optional: TRF / low complexity

# 2. heterozygous, biallelic PASS SNVs inside the mask (add an allele-fraction filter as in lesson 3 if wanted)
bcftools view -f PASS -v snps -m2 -M2 -g het -T mask.bed -Ov -o het.vcf merge_output.vcf.gz

# 3. consensus with IUPAC codes, lower case outside the mask, then 100-bp bins (seqtk 1.5; vcf2snp.pl is in psmc/utils)
seqtk seq -U asm.fa > asm.upper.fa                     # soft-masked assembly? make it upper case first
vcf2snp.pl het.vcf > het.snp                           # contig, position, ref base, IUPAC code
seqtk mutfa asm.upper.fa het.snp | seqtk seq -cM mask.bed -l80 - > diploid.fa
fq2psmcfa diploid.fa > diploid.psmcfa

A soft-masked assembly silently masks every repeat. fq2psmcfa treats every lower-case base as missing. Many assemblies are delivered soft-masked, with repeats in lower case (lesson 3, s04). seqtk mutfa keeps the case of the bases it does not change, so on a soft-masked assembly all interspersed repeats (often 30 to 50 % of a mammal genome) become N bins without anyone deciding so. Whether you want that is a decision (see below), not an accident: convert to upper case first (seqtk seq -U) and mask repeats on purpose with a BED.

Nadachowska-Brzyska et al. (2016) also give the most-cited data requirements: a mean genome coverage of at least 18×, a per-site filter of at least 10 reads, and no more than 25 % missing data. Below about 18× their curves from different birds of one population did not agree. They tested short reads on one bird family; treat the numbers as a guide, not a law.

The autosomes carry real heterozygous sites at 2.5 per kb (circles). What to notice. (1) Too loose: the crosses survive. The collapsed duplication on ctg2 and the microsatellite on ctg1 fill their bins with K; with the X kept in, its errors add Ks and its 40 bins of single-copy sequence count as homozygous. The het rate is wrong in both directions at once. (2) Even the README-style set lets a few differences of the 2-copy duplication through: its depth is about 60×, so a bound of exactly twice the mean cuts it only where the depth happens to be higher. Raise the bound to 200× and all of it comes back, with allele fractions of 0.5 and 0.33 that no allele-fraction filter can catch. (3) Too strict: narrowing the allele fraction to 0.45 to 0.55 removes real heterozygous sites while their bases stay callable, so K bins turn into T and the rate drops. A strict depth window instead turns bins into N: less data, but no bias. (4) Set the minimum depth to 1 and look at the low-coverage stretch on ctg1: bases with 3 to 9 reads count as callable but many real sites there are not seen. (5) Switch the bin rule: under the fq2psmcfa rule a bin with as few as 10 callable bases still counts as a full T; look at the bins at the edges of masked stretches.

5. fq2psmcfa and its 100-bp bins

What fq2psmcfa does, read from its source (lh3/psmc, utils/fq2psmcfa.c):

  • Every lower-case base, every N, and (in FASTQ input) every base with quality below -q counts as missing. The default is -q 10; the README uses -q20. With FASTA input there is no quality line, so -q does nothing.
  • A base counts as heterozygous if its letter stands for exactly two bases (R Y M K S W).
  • The sequence is cut into bins of 100 bases (-s). A bin becomes N if more than 90 % of its bases are missing, K if any of its non-missing bases is heterozygous, and T otherwise. A bin with 15 good bases and no heterozygous site therefore counts as a full homozygous bin; some pipelines (ours too) use a stricter rule, such as N when fewer than 50 bases are callable.
  • A whole contig is left out if fewer than 10,000 of its bases (-g) or less than 20 % of them are good. Short contigs of a fragmented assembly disappear here without a message.

Because a bin only says "at least one", two heterozygous sites 30 bases apart count once. At mammal heterozygosity this loses little; at the 2 % divergence between two species it saturates the bins, which is why the pseudo-hybrid analysis in s07 uses smaller bins.

# how much is left: bins per letter, the share of K among the non-N bins, and the share of N
grep -v '>' diploid.psmcfa | tr -d '\n' | awk '{n=gsub(/N/,"");k=gsub(/K/,"");t=gsub(/T/,""); print "N", n, "K", k, "T", t, "K share", k/(k+t), "missing", n/(n+k+t)}'

6. Checks before you run PSMC

  • How much is left. The share of N bins is your missing data. Compare it with the 25 % guide above, and report it with the callable length.
  • Heterozygosity. Het sites per callable kb, and the share of K bins, should match what you measured in lesson 3, s09. A much higher value points to false heterozygous sites; a much lower one to missed sites or unmasked missing data.
  • The X as a free control. In a male mammal (female bird), the X (Z) outside the PAR has no real heterozygous sites. Run the same filters on it: whatever heterozygous sites remain are an estimate of your false-positive rate.
  • Missed sites at low depth. The README calls not correcting for low coverage "the most common pitfall when using PSMC". If a known share of heterozygous sites is missed (the FNR), PSMC behaves as if the mutation rate were lower; psmc_plot.pl -M "sample=0.1" corrects a plot for 10 % FNR, or you divide θ0 (the scaled mutation rate PSMC estimates, s04) by (1 − FNR) in your own scaling. Li writes that he has not found a reliable way to estimate the FNR; he suggests downsampling a deep genome to the lower coverage and measuring it, but has not done this himself.
  • A second filter set. Make a second psmcfa with different but reasonable filters (for example with and without an allele-fraction filter). Parts of the curve that change between them are not supported by the data (s06).
  • File integrity. Check downloaded reads with their md5 checksum (a fingerprint of the file's content, published by the archive) and gzip -t, not by file size.
In our projects

Species A, one male: ONT reads at a depth of a few tens ×, called with Clair3 (model r1041_e82_400bps_sup_v500) on its own assembly. Mask: MAPQ ≥ 10 and depth from about 0.5 to 1.8 times the mean, closer to Li & Durbin's "half" than to the README's "third". Most of the assembly stayed callable; mitochondrion-like contigs and the half-depth contigs (the X and Y; most of their alignments fall on the X of the relative's chromosome-level assembly) were excluded. Heterozygous sites: PASS SNVs with allele fraction 0.3 to 0.7; PASS only, without the allele-fraction filter, gave slightly more K bins. The X control: after an allele-fraction filter of 0.35 to 0.65, the heterozygous sites left on the X were about 1 % of the autosomal rate. Hard quality filters changed the heterozygous-site density about seven-fold, so they were not used.

The relative (public PacBio HiFi data at a somewhat lower depth; the archive lists the library as whole-genome amplified, which is a caveat for heterozygous calls): Clair3 HiFi model, MAPQ ≥ 10, depth about 0.5 to 1.8 times the median, the same bins. The two call sets gave different curves: PASS-only gave about twice the population size of the allele-fraction set some tens of thousands of years ago. Differences between its two assembled haplotypes agreed with the read-based heterozygosity to within about 5 % (Spearman correlation close to 1 over 1-Mb windows), a check that the read calls are sound. One large read file of this species had exactly the right size but failed md5 and gzip -t; a second download was fine.
