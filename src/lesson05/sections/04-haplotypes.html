04Methylation on each parental copy

A diploid animal carries two copies of every autosome, one from its mother and one from its father. At most CpG sites both copies carry the same mark. At some places, though, one copy is methylated and the other is not, and the average over all reads then reads "50 %", a number that describes neither copy. Long reads can solve this, because one read can carry both the methylation calls and the heterozygous SNPs that show which copy it came from. This section shows how to sort reads by parental copy, how to get methylation for each copy, and what the result can and cannot tell you about the mother and the father.

The words

Haplotype
The sequence of one of the two copies of a chromosome: the alleles that sit together on the mother's copy, or on the father's.
Phasing
Working out which alleles of heterozygous sites sit together on the same copy. Result: a VCF where genotypes are written 0|1 or 1|0 (a bar instead of a slash) instead of the unphased 0/1. Heterozygous SNVs and their calling are covered in lesson 3, s09.
Phase block
A stretch in which every heterozygous site is phased relative to every other one. Between blocks the link is lost: nothing says whether block 2's "first copy" is the same parental copy as block 1's. In the VCF, the block ID is the PS (phase set) field.
Haplotagging
Labelling each read with the copy it came from, using the phased SNPs it covers. WhatsHap and LongPhase write the label as the BAM tag HP:i:1 or HP:i:2, plus PS for the block. A read that covers no heterozygous site gets no label.
Allele-specific methylation (ASM)
A site or region where the two copies differ in methylation. Its causes include parent of origin (imprinting), X inactivation, and the DNA sequence of the copy itself.
Genomic imprinting
Expression of a gene from only one parental copy, chosen by the parent it came from. It is controlled by differentially methylated regions (DMRs) whose marks are set in the egg or the sperm; the controlling DMR of a cluster of imprinted genes is called an imprinting control region (ICR).
X inactivation
In female mammals one of the two X chromosomes is silenced in each cell. In placental mammals CpG islands on the inactive X are methylated.
Simple explanation with examples: why "50 % methylated" can mean two different things

Think of a CpG site as a light switch in each cell's two copies of a chromosome. A bulk measurement only counts how many switches are on. "Half of the switches are on" can mean two very different houses:

House A (imprinted region): mother's copy ● ● ● ● ● father's copy ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ → every cell the same House B (no allele effect): mother's copy ● ○ ● ○ ● father's copy ○ ● ○ ● ○ → random per site both houses: 5 of 10 = 50 %

A short read covers one or two CpG sites and cannot tell the houses apart. A long read covers the whole region and a heterozygous SNP nearby, so it shows both the pattern (all on, or all off) and the copy it came from. Sorting reads by copy turns House A into two clean numbers, about 100 % and about 0 %, while House B stays at 50 % on both copies.

Try it

Sort toy reads by parental copy

A 3-kb toy region with 13 CpG sites (circles; filled = the read calls it methylated) in its middle, and two heterozygous SNPs on either side (squares with the base the read carries). Every read comes from one cell and one copy; half of the reads come from each copy, and for the X the cells are drawn in exact proportion to the slider, so the toy shows the expected values without sampling noise. Methylation calls are wrong 4 % of the time. Phasing off: all reads are pooled, as a plain pileup would do. Phasing on: reads that cover a SNP are tagged HP1 or HP2 by the base they carry; reads that miss both SNPs stay untagged. Pick a region type, then compare the bars at the bottom.

Phasing

What to notice. (1) With phasing off, the imprinted region reads about 50 %, but look at the reads themselves: almost every read is all filled or all empty, never half and half. That pattern is already a hint that two states are mixed. (2) Turn phasing on: the same reads split into about 100 % and about 0 %. (3) Shorten the reads below about 1 kb: more reads miss both SNPs and stay grey, so each copy rests on fewer reads. (4) "X in a female" at 50 % gives about 50 % on both copies, because each cell silences a randomly chosen X; only when inactivation is skewed (move the slider) do the copies separate. (5) "No allele effect" stays at 50 % on both copies, and each read is a mix of filled and empty circles. (6) Press "new sample" a few times: which copy is called HP1 changes, because the HP number is not tied to the mother or the father.

Where parent-specific methylation shows up in animals

  • Imprinted genes. Imprinting has been found in placental mammals and in marsupials, and not (as yet) in the egg-laying monotremes (Renfree et al. 2009, 2013). Marsupials have fewer imprinted loci, and some marsupial imprinted genes have no DMR at all and are silenced by histone marks instead; H19, IGF2 and PEG10 do have DMRs in marsupials (Renfree et al. 2008, 2013). The marks are set in the germline, so in a body tissue an ICR is expected to be close to 100 % on one copy and close to 0 % on the other. Gigante et al. (2019) found known ICRs in mouse samples from nanopore reads at an average coverage of about 10×.
  • X inactivation in females. In placental mammals each cell silences one X at random, and CpG islands on the silenced X are methylated (Loebel & Johnston 1996; Peeters et al. 2016). Because the choice is random, a bulk tissue mixes cells that silenced the mother's X with cells that silenced the father's, and both copies average about 50 % at an X CpG island unless the tissue is skewed (most cells chose the same X; Peeters et al. 2016 discuss causes, including the mouse Xce locus). Rodent extra-embryonic membranes and all marsupial tissues silence the father's X, but Loebel & Johnston (1996) found an X-linked CpG island of the common wallaroo unmethylated in both males and females, and concluded that marsupial X inactivation does not need CpG island methylation.
  • Sequence-dependent ASM. Many allelic methylation differences follow the DNA sequence of the copy (for example a SNP that destroys a CpG or a transcription-factor site), not the parent. Onuchic et al. (2018) found thousands of such loci in human tissues. In one animal you cannot tell this from imprinting: both give one methylated and one unmethylated haplotype. Parents' genomes, or the same locus in several individuals, separate the two.

The workflow

  1. Aligned reads with methylation tags
    A sorted, indexed BAM whose reads still carry their MM/ML tags (modification tags) after alignment (s03).
    dorado / samtools fastq -T + minimap2 -y · pbmm2
  2. Call heterozygous SNVs
    On the same reads or on better ones from the same animal (lesson 3, s09).
    Clair3 · DeepVariant
  3. Phase the SNVs
    Long reads link neighbouring heterozygous sites into phase blocks.
    whatshap phase · longphase phase
  4. Haplotag the reads
    Each read gets HP and PS tags; its methylation tags are kept.
    whatshap haplotag · longphase haplotag
  5. Pile up methylation per haplotype
    One bedMethyl table per copy, plus one with all reads.
    modkit pileup --phased · methbat pileup · aligned_bam_to_cpg_scores (PacBio)
  6. Compare the copies
    Within each phase block, regions where HP1 and HP2 differ by a lot; check known imprinted genes first.
    your own script · modkit dmr (s06) · IGV (s07)
# whatshap 2.8. One sample, read groups not trusted: --ignore-read-groups.
# --reference is "highly recommended" for error-prone long reads (WhatsHap guide).
whatshap phase -o phased.vcf --reference asm.fa --ignore-read-groups variants.vcf.gz reads.bam
bgzip phased.vcf && tabix -p vcf phased.vcf.gz        # haplotag needs .vcf.gz with an index
whatshap stats --tsv=phase_stats.tsv phased.vcf.gz     # fraction of het SNVs phased, block count and lengths

# Tag reads HP:i:1 / HP:i:2 and PS; use the same --reference as for phase
whatshap haplotag -o hap.bam --reference asm.fa --ignore-read-groups \
  --output-threads 4 --output-haplotag-list haplotags.tsv phased.vcf.gz reads.bam
samtools index hap.bam
samtools view -c -d HP:1 hap.bam; samtools view -c -d HP:2 hap.bam   # reads per copy

# modkit 0.6.4: --phased writes hp1.bedmethyl, hp2.bedmethyl and combined.bedmethyl
# (before v0.6.0 this was --partition-tag HP; the docs say --phased replaces it)
modkit pileup hap.bam by_hap/ --phased --cpg --modified-bases 5mC 5hmC \
  --ref asm.fa -t 16 --log-filepath pileup.log

In the bedMethyl output, column 10 is the number of valid calls at the site and column 11 the percent modified (modkit documentation). Untagged reads are counted only in combined.bedmethyl, so hp1 + hp2 coverage is lower than combined coverage. modkit uses only primary alignments.

What can go wrong

  • HP1 is not "the mother". WhatsHap sets HP from the order of alleles in the phased genotype (1|0 means haplotype 1 carries the ALT allele). That order is arbitrary in each phase block. HP1 in block 1 and HP1 in block 2 can be different parents. modkit's --phased splits by HP only, so a genome-wide hp1.bedmethyl is a patchwork of both parents (our reading of the two documentations). Compare HP1 with HP2 within a block, never across blocks.
  • Parent of origin needs extra information. Parents' reads (a trio), or known imprinted DMRs used as anchors: Akbari et al. (2023) assigned the parent of origin of whole human chromosomes from nanopore methylation at known imprinted loci plus Strand-seq phasing, without parental DNA. For a wild species without a list of imprinted loci, use parents' genomes, or say that HP1/HP2 is unassigned.
  • Each copy gets half the depth. At 30× total, each copy has about 15× minus untagged reads. Summarise over regions (a DMR, a CpG island), not single CpGs.
  • Hemizygous X and Y. In a male, the X has one copy and no heterozygous sites, so nothing can be phased there; find these contigs by half depth (lesson 2, s12). The same holds for the Z in female birds.
  • Tissue is a mixture. Imprinted loci vary little between tissues (Akbari et al. 2023), but other ASM can differ between cell types, and X inactivation is a mosaic. A 50/50 per-copy result on the X is expected, not a failure.
  • Wrong phasing looks like weaker ASM. A switch error in the middle of a DMR mixes the copies and pulls both towards 50 %. A DMR that is 90/10 on one side of a block boundary and 50/50 on the other is worth a look in IGV, coloured by HP and PS (as the WhatsHap guide shows).

Methylation can also help phasing and assembly checks

The logic of the toy runs the other way, too. Where heterozygous SNPs are too sparse to link two phase blocks, CpG sites whose methylation differs between the copies can act as extra "alleles". MethPhaser (Fu et al. 2024) does this on SNV-phased nanopore data and reports a 78 % to 151 % longer phase-block N50 at a phasing accuracy of 83.4 % to 98.7 % on human cell lines. LongPhase does it in one step: longphase modcall calls allele-specific 5mC sites from MM/ML tags, and longphase phase --mod-file phases them together with the SNPs; its README reports longer blocks with no rise in switch errors at 60×. For a diploid assembly, methylation at known imprinted regions is how Akbari et al. (2023) gave whole haplotypes a mother or father label; for an assembly with hap1 and hap2 (lesson 2, s07) the same idea could label them, but only in species where imprinted regions are known (our inference; we have not tried it).

# LongPhase 2.1.2: SNP + 5mC co-phasing (README example; output names follow -o)
longphase modcall -b reads.bam -r asm.fa -t 8 -o modcall
longphase phase -s snps.vcf --mod-file modcall.vcf -b reads.bam -r asm.fa -t 8 -o phased --ont   # --pb for HiFi

Both tools were tested on human data. MethPhaser's authors write that the concept "can also be extended to non-human diploid genomes"; we know of no test in a wild species.

In our projects

Species A (a rodent): WhatsHap phased nearly all heterozygous SNPs from our long reads, with blocks spanning most of each contig (contigs tens of Mb long were often covered by only one or two blocks). Haplotype-resolved methylation would therefore be feasible with these reads if they had methylation calls. They do not: the FASTQ we received carries no basecaller-model tags, we have no raw signal files, and the reads are probably from a CycloneSEQ instrument rather than Oxford Nanopore (see s02). The animal is also a male: a large set of contigs at half depth are its single X, so it could not show X inactivation even with methylation data; a female would be needed.

Practising without our data. Use public human data. Oxford Nanopore's 2023 release of the Genome in a Bottle samples (ONT Open Data, s3://ont-open-data/giab_2023.05/) has raw signal (pod5) for HG002 (a son), his father HG003 and mother HG004, and HG001 (GM12878, a woman according to Coriell). Under analysis/variant_calling/ each sample also has a ready-made haplotagged BAM (tagged with WhatsHap 1.7, according to its header) whose reads carry MM/ML tags (we checked the start of the HG002 file), and per-haplotype CpG tables hap1.methyl.cpg.bed.gz and hap2.methyl.cpg.bed.gz. With the parents' data you can check which HP is which parent at a known imprinted region, and HG001 gives the X of a female. They are cell lines, not tissues. The HG002 BAM is large (60× coverage): start with one region (samtools view reads remote indexed files). The pb-CpG-tools README points to a haplotagged HiFi BAM of HG002 with 5mC tags, but that link returned "Not Found" when we checked (31 October 2026).

Simple explanation with examples: reading the per-copy result

Suppose a region gives, inside one phase block, hp1 92 %, hp2 6 %, combined 49 %. One copy is methylated and the other is not. What you know: this animal has allele-specific methylation here. What you do not know from these numbers alone: whether HP1 is the mother's copy, and whether the difference comes from the parent (imprinting) or from the sequence of HP1 itself.

If the same region gives hp1 48 %, hp2 52 %: no allele effect, or (on the X of a female) random inactivation. If it gives hp1 90 %, hp2 10 % on one side of a PS boundary and 10 %, 90 % on the other: the labels swapped at the block boundary, which is normal.
